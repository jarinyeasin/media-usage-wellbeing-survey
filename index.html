Key findings
Sleep disruption (β=0.436) is the strongest predictor of overall wellbeing impact — far stronger than raw screentime (β=-0.027). The model explains 46.9% of variance (R²=0.469). This suggests how media affects behaviour matters more than how many hours are spent on it.
22 students (37%) fall in the High-Risk cluster — characterised by very high social comparison (mean 3.4/7), sleep disruption (6.2/7), and wellbeing impact (5.9/7). The largest group is Moderate Impact at 15 students (25%).
Concentration impairment is the most reported symptom — Q4 (difficulty concentrating) has a mean of 5.20/7, the highest of all 11 items, above even overall wellbeing impact (4.98/7).

Methods & limitations

Data: Primary survey via Google Forms, n=60 Bangladeshi university students (38 female, 22 male), collected Feb 2026. Age range 17–37 years (mean=24.0). Methods: Descriptive statistics · Pearson correlation · Welch t-test · One-way ANOVA · Multiple linear regression · K-means (k=3, StandardScaler) · PCA (2 components). Stack: Python 3 · pandas · NumPy · scikit-learn · scipy · matplotlib. Limitations: Convenience sample limits generalisability. Self-reported screentime may be underestimated. Cross-sectional design precludes causal inference.
